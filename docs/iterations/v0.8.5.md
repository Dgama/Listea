# Listea v0.8.5 — a Task whose later questions depend on earlier answers

> **Status:** implemented 2026-10-29 and device-accepted; the build is `0.8.5-preview` (805),
> unreleased. Schema 34, protocol `listea.package/3` and export v7. The walk, its evidence and every
> deviation are in [the acceptance record](#acceptance-record).
> **Baseline:** v0.8.4, `0.8.4-preview` (804), accepted and pushed 2026-10-28, schema 33, export v6,
> `listea.package/2`.
> **Amends:** [v0.8.4](v0.8.4.md) D3/D8/D9 and its completion section. v0.8.4 said conditions live
> only inside one Prompt and that an explicit completion is never silently cleared. v0.8.5 puts a
> condition between parts, and reopens a completed Task when a required part becomes active again.
> Both amendments are recorded at the point they arise in the v0.8.4 page and repeated below.

## At a glance

v0.8.5 answers one question:

> **Can one Task's later decisions depend on the answers to its earlier ones, when the decisions
> are different kinds of question?**

Yes. A TaskPart may declare **when it applies**, based on one field of an earlier required part.
A part is therefore `pending`, `active`, or `inactive` in addition to being answered or not. The
pages of an inactive part stay in the page list, masked and explained; the person can still walk
past them, and the queue's Task count never changes.

The round also makes a Task's parts genuinely **heterogeneous**: each part may carry its own
**judgment** (the template that decides how its answer commits), while the Task keeps one completion
policy. A yes/no form can gate a PDF review, an image/video review, a vocabulary card, a dating
card, and an ordinary questionnaire in the same Task, with one add-on at the end.

The vocabulary this round adds:

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Conditional part** | A TaskPart whose applicability is declared by a `when` condition on an earlier part. |
| **Applicability** | A part's derived state: `pending` (not decidable yet), `active`, or `inactive`. |
| **Part judgment** | The template that governs one part's commit, revisability and answer sheet. Stored in `task_parts.partJudgment`; null inherits the Task-level definition. |
| **Stale answer** | An answer kept in history and state but no longer counted, because its part became inactive, or was reactivated and has not been reconfirmed. |
| **Masked page** | A page that remains in the page list but is non-interactive and shows an explanation card instead of its body. |

The words **review** and **Task Review** keep their v0.8.4 meaning (the Task-level template and its
history). The per-part column is deliberately **judgment**, not a second review: one answer sheet per
part, one governing policy for the Task.

## Why this round

v0.8.4 made a Task hold 1..N TaskParts and put the conditional questionnaire inside a single
`form` Prompt. That boundary was correct for the first multi-part round and wrong for the next one:

1. **A conditional chain was one interface.** Every question in the chain had to be a field of one
   `form` body, so every question had to be a type that form Viewer could draw. The user's example —
   "Did you eat?" → "Here is the menu, pick the dishes" — needs a PDF, not a text field.
2. **The taker's page model already had the right shape.** A TaskPart is a decision with its own
   pages, answer and template. Splitting the chain into parts removes the one-interface constraint
   without inventing a second page model.
3. **Hiding a field made the page count lie.** A form's internal `when` hides a field but not a
   page; the person sees a shorter form. A Task part that disappears would change the page list, the
   counter and the queue guide on every surface. The round fixes the count instead: conditional
   pages always exist, and only their **usable state** changes.
4. **The Task had only one template.** A Task with a form part and a PDF part cannot be judged by
   one Headless Template. The Task needs one **completion** policy and one **judgment** per part.

The user's route was explicit: first split the conditionally bound questions into their own parts
and keep the questionnaire family; second, allow the bound questions to be other kinds of work. This
round is that two-step route. It does not add a general expression language, cross-Task conditions,
or a new editor.

## Decisions

Each decision is **[settled]** for the round unless marked **[open]**.

### D1. Conditions live between parts, in the Package [settled]

A Prompt may declare one `when` condition referencing an **earlier** Prompt:

```json
{
  "promptKey": "menu",
  "kind": "media",
  "schema": 1,
  "response": "freeform",
  "title": "Choose the dishes",
  "description": "prompts/menu.json",
  "files": [{"member": "menu.pdf", "name": "Menu"}],
  "section": "meal",
  "when": {"prompt": "ate", "field": "ate", "in": ["yes"]}
}
```

- The condition is the **setter's declaration of applicability**. It says which answer makes the
  Prompt apply; it does not say which Task holds it, or whether it is required.
- The condition is evaluated **within one Task**. The source Prompt must be a part of the same Task,
  must be earlier, and must be required. A composition that splits the source and the target makes
  the target unavailable (`condition-source-outside-task`) rather than silently answering a question
  about another Task.
- One condition per Prompt. A condition has one source field and `equals` or `in` values, the same
  spelling the `form` Viewer already uses internally. Chains compose because parts are ordered:
  a target can itself be the source of a later condition.
- Conditions cycle-free by construction: a source is an earlier Prompt. A forward reference is a
  manifest diagnostic, not a runtime guess.
- **Cross-Task conditions remain out of scope.** A later Task depending on an earlier Task's answer
  still needs a producer follow-up Task or a future List-level rule.

This reverses v0.8.4's "conditions are inside the Prompt body only". The v0.8.4 boundary was a
scope decision for that round, not a claim that cross-Prompt conditions were impossible. The
v0.8.4 page records the amendment.

### D2. Applicability is a derived part state [settled]

Every present part has one applicability state:

| State | Meaning | Can the part be answered? |
|---|---|---|
| `pending` | The condition cannot be decided yet: the source is unanswered, or the source is itself pending. | No; the page is masked. |
| `active` | No condition, or the source's current answer satisfies it. | Yes, if the part is otherwise available. |
| `inactive` | The source is active and answered, and its answer does **not** satisfy the condition; or the source is inactive (propagation). | No; the page is masked. |

Applicability is **derived** from the parts' stored conditions, their current responses, and the
parts' order. It is not a stored column: the current response is the truth, and a stored copy would
be a second truth to keep in step. The evaluation is a pure function over the Task's parts and part
states, and runs at read time and around every write that can change an answer or a part set.

The rules:

- No condition → `active`.
- A source that is `inactive` → the target is `inactive`, regardless of any stale answer the source
  still holds. Inactivity propagates down the chain.
- A source that is `pending` → the target is `pending`.
- A source that is `active` but unanswered → the target is `pending`.
- A source that is `active` and answered but does not carry the named field → the target is
  `inactive`. A field hidden by the source's own internal branch cannot satisfy a downstream
  condition.
- Otherwise the target is `active` when the field's current value is in the condition's values, and
  `inactive` when it is not.

A source must be a required part. An optional source could never be answered, leaving its target
`pending` forever; the import diagnoses that combination instead of letting a required target block
on an optional gate.

Availability and applicability are orthogonal. A part may be `unavailable` and `inactive`; inactive
wins for completion, and the page still says why it is not usable. A detached part is not in the
page list and no longer evaluates.

### D3. The page list is stable; conditional pages are masked [settled]

Every present part contributes its pages in composition order, whether it is active, pending, or
inactive:

```text
for each present part in composition order:
    Content(partKey)       if the part has a payload
    Asset(partKey, asset)  for each Asset
AddOn                      if present
```

- An inactive or pending part's Content page is replaced by an **explanation card**.
- Its Asset pages keep their place and their file identity, but are drawn masked with the same
  explanation: no zoom, no video transport, no marks, no actions, no commit. The person can swipe
  past them exactly as they can walk the rest of the Task.
- The Add-on is never masked and never conditional. It stays after the last part.
- The count does not move when an answer changes. A Task of eight questions shows eight pages even
  when two of them do not apply. A Task with a PDF part shows the part's PDF pages too; the inner
  counter counts **pages**, not parts.
- **A TaskPart is not a counter level.** The outer `x(m/n)/y` still means Task position and page
  position; the catalog still counts questions/parts and files. TaskPart remains the app's and the
  setter's word, not a number the taker has to learn.

This is the user's "always exist, only usable or visible state changes" rule, made concrete for
page-based content. Masking is not hiding: the page, the file name and the reason are still on
screen.

### D4. An inactive answer goes stale; a reactivated required part reopens the Task [settled]

- When a part with an answer becomes `inactive` (active → inactive), its answer is kept but marked
  **stale**. It remains visible, remains in history, and is exported with `stale: true`, but it is
  not counted as an answered part for completion.
- When a part becomes `active` again (inactive → active), a stale answer stays stale and is shown as
  **the previous answer**. The part is not counted as answered until the person submits it again.
  The page says so; the previous answer is a starting point, not a silent pass.
- A stale part may be submitted again even when its judgment is non-revisable. Non-revisability
  forbids a second answer to a question that is still current; it cannot forbid the one
  reconfirmation a branch change requires. After that commit the part is answered and non-revisable
  again.
- If a completed Task has a **required** part whose applicability changes from `inactive` to
  `active`, the Task **reopens**: `isCompleted` becomes false, regardless of whether the completion
  was automatic or explicit. This is the one exception to v0.8.4's "an explicit completion is never
  silently cleared": the required set grew after the person signed off, and the sign-off no longer
  describes the Task they saw.
- A `pending → active` transition does not by itself reopen an explicitly completed Task. The part
  was not closed before, it was undecided; it becomes answerable and the person may uncomplete the
  Task. Automatic completion still recomputes and can reopen on any loss of satisfaction.
- Shrinking the required set (active → inactive) never reopens a completed Task. The Task may remain
  complete if the remaining active required parts are satisfied; an explicit completion stays
  explicit.
- Reopening is recorded in `task_state.completionSource = "reopened"` (a new value beside
  `automatic` and `explicit`), so the stored state says how the checkbox came to be where it is.
  It is deliberately **not** a `review_events` row: that table is an append-only log of submissions,
  and its newest row is read as the Task's latest response — a non-submission row there would be
  mistaken for an answer. The page that reactivated states why, and `completionSource` is what a
  later reader consults.
- Reopening a Task reopens its List exactly as unchecking a Task does today. A completion export
  that already happened is history; it is not retracted.

### D5. Completion is aggregated over active required parts [settled]

The Task-level completion policy now reads "required" as **required and active**:

| Policy | Meaning with applicability |
|---|---|
| `allRequiredParts` | Every required part that is `active` is answered and not stale, and no active required part is unavailable. Inactive required parts do not block. With no active required parts the policy is vacuously satisfied. |
| `anyPart` | At least one required active part is answered and not stale. If no required part is active, automatic completion cannot happen. |
| `explicitOnly` | Answering never completes; the completion control is the only path. Reopening under D4 still applies. |
| `singlePart` | Legacy: the one part's commit completes the Task as before. A single-part Task cannot hold its own condition source, so applicability does not change this policy. |

- An inactive required part does not block, and a stale answer does not satisfy.
- A `pending` required part implies its source is unanswered or pending; the source is required, so
  the Task cannot complete until the source is answered. The count shown by the completion control
  counts active required parts, not pending ones; the source is already in that count.
- The completion control's pending count excludes inactive parts and includes stale answered parts.
  The confirmation dialog says how many required questions are still open.

### D6. Every part may carry its own judgment [settled]

A TaskPart may name a **part judgment**: the template that decides how its answer commits, which
intents it accepts, whether it is revisable, and whether a commit advances. It is stored in
`task_parts.partJudgment` as a `TemplateVersionRef`; null means **inherit the Task-level
`task_imports.taskReview`**.

- The Task-level definition keeps the **completion policy**. A part judgment's `completion` field is
  ignored: completion is one Task fact, not a per-part fact.
- A part judgment's `completesTask` is honoured only under the Task-level `singlePart` policy; a
  multi-part Task uses `allRequiredParts`/`anyPart`/`explicitOnly` and ignores it.
- The column is called **part judgment**, not a second review. The docs and UI may call it the
  part's **answer sheet** when speaking to a person.
- A missing or unreadable judgment ref makes the part unavailable (`unsupported-judgment`), not
  silently inherited. Fail closed.
- A mixed Task gets every part's judgment resolved at import and stored. A homogeneous Task may
  leave every part's column null and inherit the Task-level definition, which preserves v0.8.4
  behavior exactly.

### D7. A Task can mix kinds, and a media part can be judged [settled]

A Package Prompt may now be a **media** Prompt:

- `kind: "media"`, `schema: 1`, `response: "freeform"`, a body `{"stem": "…"}`, and one or more
  `files`.
- The `media`/1 Viewer draws the stem as the part's Content page; the files become its Asset pages
  and are reviewed with the existing image, video and PDF surfaces, marks and actions.
- A media part's answer is **answered-empty**: pressing its part judgment's Done control commits the
  part with no typed result. The files, marks and actions live under the part and are exported
  there. There is no invented choice or point.
- A media part is a condition **target**, not a source: freeform has no named answer field.
- The built-in `builtin.media.part@1` judgment provides the Done control. It is used for media and
  degenerate file/folder parts **inside a multi-part Task**. A single-part media Task keeps its
  current Task-level template and completion checkbox, byte for byte.

The other kinds keep their answer sheets:

| Part kind | Part judgment resolved for a mixed Task | Answer sheet |
|---|---|---|
| `form` | `builtin.structured.form@1` | fields + Submit |
| `profile` | `builtin.structured.single-choice@1` | option tap commits |
| `vocabulary` | `builtin.structured.single-choice@1` | option tap commits |
| `media` (freeform) | `builtin.media.part@1` | files/marks/actions + Done |
| degenerate file/folder part in a multi-part Task | `builtin.media.part@1` | files/marks/actions + Done |
| homogeneous multi-part Package Task | null per part; inherit Task-level | as v0.8.4 |
| mixed multi-part Package Task's Task level | `builtin.structured.mixed@1`, `allRequiredParts` | container only |

The `mixed` Task-level template is a **completion container**: it has no viewer of its own, no
buttons beyond the completion checkbox, and its parts' own judgments. Its `viewer` reference is a
placeholder the resolution never uses for a part with a judgment.

### D8. Protocol v3 [settled]

`listea.package/3` adds the condition and the media Prompt to the v2 grammar:

- Top level: unchanged from v2 (`protocol`, `packageKey`, `revision`, `title`, `prompts`,
  `resources`, `sections`).
- `prompts[].when`: one condition object, optional.
  - `prompt`: an earlier Prompt's `promptKey`.
  - `field`: a named answer the source exposes. A `choice.single` Prompt exposes `choice`; a `form`
    Prompt exposes its field keys; a `media` Prompt exposes none.
  - `equals` or `in`: exactly one spelling, as in the form Viewer.
- `response: "freeform"` is accepted only for `kind: "media"`; the Viewer declares the contract and
  the protocol does not parse the body. Other freeform kinds remain unsupported.
- `kind: "media"`, schema 1, requires a non-empty `stem` body and at least one file. A media Prompt
  with no file is a diagnostic.
- Conditions are validated against the source body through the Viewer capability's answer-field
  surface, not by Core learning a Viewer's vocabulary. A source that is later, missing, optional in
  the chosen composition, or does not expose the field is diagnosed.
- v1 and v2 remain readable under their own grammars. v3-only fields in a v2 manifest are errors
  (`when-in-v2`, `media-in-v2`; a freeform body under a pre-v3 protocol is the existing
  `response-unsupported`); a v3 manifest that uses `assets` is `assets-in-v3`. A v0.8.4 reader
  rejects a v3 Package whole, as it should.
- The manifest fingerprint includes the condition, so changing a condition is a content change.

The exact grammar and diagnostics freeze in [the plan](v0.8.5-plan.md).

### D9. Schema 34 [settled]

Schema 34 adds three part-level facts, all additive:

- `task_parts.condition TEXT` — the canonical condition, stored on the Task so applicability does
  not need the Package to be present.
- `task_parts.partJudgment TEXT` — the part's template reference; null inherits.
- `task_part_state.stale INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 0` — whether the current answer is kept but not
  counted.

Existing rows get `condition = NULL`, `partJudgment = NULL`, `stale = 0`. A schema-33 Task has no
conditions, so its applicability is `active` everywhere and its pages, responses and export are
unchanged. No old event or answer is reinterpreted.

The schema deliberately adds **no** completion column: a reopen writes
`task_state.completionSource = "reopened"` in the column v0.8.4 already has. Column names follow the
project's camelCase Room convention (`partKey`, `promptKey`, `schemaVersion`), so the DDL is
`condition`, `partJudgment`, `stale`.

### D10. Export v7 [settled]

A Task that has a condition or a part judgment exports **version 7**. A compatible v0.8.4 Task still
exports v6; a compatible single-part Task still exports v5. The version line is the branch, as it
was for v5 and v6.

Each `parts[]` entry gains:

- `judgment`: `{id, version, name?}` or null (inherit the Task's template),
- `when`: the condition object or null,
- `applicability`: `"pending" | "active" | "inactive"`,
- `stale`: whether the answer is not counted.

`result` keeps its v6 meaning: pending is `null`, answered-empty is `""`, a typed answer is an
object. An inactive part that holds an old answer exports it with `stale: true`; an inactive part
with no answer exports `null`. The Task's `completed` stays authoritative.

The export is a **snapshot plus the logic**: downstream receives the condition, the source
`promptKey`, the resolved applicability and the result. It can replay the function, and it can see
an answer that no longer counts without having to guess. This is the response/logic linkage the user
asked for: the Package's function is recorded with the answer sheets, not encoded as a hidden
per-answer attribute.

### D11. Counts, queue and surfaces [settled]

- The outer counter (`x(m/n)/y`) counts **Tasks** and **pages**; a conditional part never becomes a
  queue entry and never changes `y`.
- The inner `m/n` counts every page, masked included. For an eight-question Task it reads `/8`
  whether two questions apply or not. For a Task holding a PDF, the denominator includes the PDF
  pages; the catalog and the part guide are where questions are counted.
- The catalog counts every present part as a question/part, including inactive ones, and counts files
  separately. A conditional question is still a question the setter asked.
- The add-on is not a page count on the catalog and not a queue entry; it remains after the parts.
- Quick Review and List Review build their queues from Tasks, not parts; an inactive part does not
  remove its Task, and a Task with all parts inactive still counts once.
- All five viewing surfaces use the shared page list and the shared applicability result. Previews
  show the same masks and explanation cards as Review, read-only; the queue menu/jump entries are
  still Tasks and land on the Task's first page (masked or not).
- Reopening a Task affects the **next** queue built. An already-open round does not insert a Task
  it did not start with; that is recorded as an open item.

### D12. Version and scope [settled]

- The round is **v0.8.5**; the build is `0.8.5-preview` (versionCode 805) until acceptance.
- Schema 34, protocol v3 and export v7 are the round's contracts.
- No tag, release or push without the user's explicit instruction. The implementation follows the
  plan in docs → main source → test layers.
- The round is a framework round within 0.8, not the v0.9 product layer. It adds no editing UI for
  conditions, no expression language, no cross-Task logic and no custom composition.

## The model

```text
Task
├── TaskPart (gate)                         applicability: active
│   ├── payload: form                        judgment: builtin.structured.form@1
│   ├── current response: {ate: "yes"}       condition: none
│   └── pages: Content
├── TaskPart (menu)                         applicability: active | inactive | pending
│   ├── payload: media ("Choose the dishes") judgment: builtin.media.part@1
│   ├── files: menu.pdf                     condition: gate.ate in ["yes"]
│   ├── current response: "" (answered)      stale: false
│   └── pages: Content, Asset(menu.pdf)
├── TaskPart (words)                        applicability: active
│   ├── payload: vocabulary                 judgment: builtin.structured.single-choice@1
│   ├── current response: option "know"     condition: gate.ate in ["yes"]
│   └── pages: Content
├── ...
├── Task-level completion policy: allRequiredParts
└── Add-on (optional, Task-level, never conditional)
```

The condition is an edge from an earlier part to a later one. The edge is part of the Package's
declaration and is stored on the TaskPart; the **taker** still decides which Prompts form one Task.
A condition that crosses the taker's Task boundary is a composition that cannot be honoured, and the
import says so.

## Applicability in detail

### Evaluation

The evaluator is pure and ordered:

```text
evaluate(parts, states):
  result = {}
  for part in parts by position:
    if not part.present: continue
    condition = part.condition
    if condition == null:
      result[part] = Active
      continue
    source = parts.firstOrNull { it.partKey == condition.sourcePartKey }
    if source == null:                 # composition split or detached source
      result[part] = Inactive         # availability carries the diagnostic; plan P1 settles this
      continue
    when sourceApplicability(result[source]) is:
      Inactive -> result[part] = Inactive
      Pending  -> result[part] = Pending
      Active   ->
        response = states[source].response
        if response is null:           result[part] = Pending
        else:
          value = fieldValue(source, response, condition.fieldKey)
          result[part] = if value in condition.values then Active else Inactive
  return result
```

`fieldValue` reads the same canonical answer the export reads:

- `choice.single`: the newest `Choice` event's option id, exposed as field `choice`.
- `form`: the visible `FieldAnswer` whose key matches; a choice field's option id, a text field's
  text.
- `media`: no fields; a condition on it is refused at import.

A missing field is **inactive**, not pending: the source has answered, and its answer does not make
the target apply. This is what lets a condition sit downstream of another condition inside a form.

A missing source is also **inactive**, not pending. The import has already marked such a target
unavailable (composition split, detached part), and an orphaned condition must not sit waiting on an
answer that cannot come. This is the plan's ruling; an earlier draft of this design said pending.

### Examples

**The meal tree**

```text
gate: form, fields ate=yes|no
menu:  media, when gate.ate in [yes]
words: vocabulary, when gate.ate in [yes]
```

- `gate` unanswered: every target is `pending`; all pages exist, all masked with "Answer the gate
  first".
- `gate` = `yes`: targets `active`; the PDF, the card, the options are usable.
- `gate` = `no`: targets `inactive`; the same pages remain, masked with "You answered No; this does
  not apply".
- Change `yes` → `no` after answering the PDF: the PDF part becomes inactive, its answer becomes
  stale, and if the Task was complete it reopens (because the PDF part was required and active
  before). Change back to `yes`: the PDF part is active but stale; Done is required again.

**A chain**

```text
ate:  form, field ate=yes|no
menu: media, when ate.ate = yes
wine: form,  when menu.choice = red      (only if menu exposes a field)
```

If `menu` is a media part it exposes no field, so `wine` cannot depend on it. A chain through a
media part is not available in v0.8.5; a chain through a form or a single-choice part is. This is a
deliberate limit: freeform media has no canonical scalar, and inventing one would make the PDF's
marks into a decision field they are not.

**Inactive propagation**

```text
ate:  form, field ate=yes|no
menu: media, when ate.ate = yes
note: form, when menu.choice = ...      (invalid if menu is media)
```

For a valid chain, if `menu` is a form that becomes inactive, `note` is inactive even if `menu` has
an old answer. An old answer in a closed branch cannot reopen a downstream branch.

### Source requirements

- Earlier `position` than the target.
- Required in the Task. The import refuses a condition whose source is optional in the chosen
  composition (`condition-source-optional`).
- Same Task. The import marks the target unavailable (`condition-source-outside-task`) when the
  chosen composition splits it, and the import report warns before and after.
- The source exposes the named field. The planner asks the Viewer capability for the field set; a
  source body whose field does not exist is a diagnostic.
- A `media` Prompt is never a source.

## Part judgments

### Storage and resolution

`task_parts.partJudgment` holds a template reference (the existing `TemplateVersionRef` encoding).
The read path resolves it through the same resolver the Task-level review uses. A null column means
"inherit the Task-level `task_imports.taskReview`".

At import, the resolver for a Task with more than one part:

1. If every part is the same supported kind (all form, all single-choice), leave the columns null;
   the Task-level definition applies to every part, exactly as v0.8.4 does.
2. Otherwise, resolve each part's built-in judgment from its kind/response:
   `form` → form, `profile`/`vocabulary` → single-choice, `media`/degenerate file/folder with a
   multi-part Task → media part.
3. Set the Task-level definition to `builtin.structured.mixed@1` with
   `completion = allRequiredParts`, unless the user explicitly chose a Task Review with another
   policy. The Task-level definition's viewer/accepts/commit are a container; part judgments own the
   answer sheets.

The column is nullable so a v0.8.4 Task and a homogeneous v0.8.5 Task read exactly as they did.

### The media part

`builtin.media.part@1`:

- viewer: the media viewer (`builtin.media.viewer@1`),
- accepts: no viewer events,
- commit: `ConfirmControl("done")`, a **Done** button in the template's buttons,
- revisable: true,
- completesTask: true (ignored under a multi-part Task policy),
- advanceAfterCommit: true.

Pressing Done commits the part. The commit writes the part's current response as answered-empty and
leaves the Asset marks/actions where they are. The Task policy decides whether that was the last
answer. A single-part media Task does not use this judgment; it keeps the Task-level template and
checkbox.

### The mixed container

`builtin.structured.mixed@1`:

- name: "Mixed Task",
- buttons: empty; the Task-level completion control is the completion checkbox,
- viewer: `builtin.viewer.mixed@1`, a headless reference that is never used for a part with its own
  judgment,
- accepts: empty,
- commit: the completion checkbox,
- revisable: true,
- completesTask: true,
- advanceAfterCommit: true,
- completion: `allRequiredParts`.

It is a policy container, not a renderer. A part page resolves its own judgment; the Task bar
resolves the container for completion and for the explicit control. The template's own
`completesTask`/`advanceAfterCommit` flags describe a headless container's default behaviour and are
inert under `allRequiredParts`; the plan settles them as `true` so the template is internally
consistent when resolved outside a Task.

## Pages and the explanation card

### Page list

The page list does not filter by applicability. Detached parts are excluded as before; unavailable
parts remain with their diagnostic; inactive and pending parts remain masked. The add-on remains
last.

### The card

A masked page shows an explanation card. The card is built from the condition, not from a stored
UI string:

| Situation | Card says | Action |
|---|---|---|
| `pending`, source unanswered | "Answer ‘Did you eat?’ first. This question depends on it." | jump to the source's first page |
| `pending`, source pending | the same, naming the first unanswered gate in the chain | jump to that gate |
| `inactive` | "You answered ‘No’ to ‘Did you eat?’. This question applies when the answer is ‘Yes’." | jump to the source |
| `stale` after reactivation | "This question applies again because you changed ‘Did you eat?’. Your earlier answer is shown but will not count until you submit again." | the page is active; show the previous answer and the part's commit control |

The card names the source Prompt's title, the field's label, the answer's label, and the condition's
accepted values. Those labels are resolved at read time from the source's stored payload, the same
way the export resolves labels; if a label is unavailable, the key/id is shown. The card never
records an answer and never changes eligibility.

### Navigation

- Inner arrows and swipes walk every page, masked included.
- A masked Content page is not interactive, so the Review surface's "interactive Content pages do not
  swipe" rule does not apply to it; a masked Asset page keeps the ordinary page-turn behavior but
  suppresses the viewer's own gestures.
- The "jump to source" action is a page-index assignment to the source part's first page. It does
  not change the queue entry or the resume cursor by itself.
- The queue menu lists Tasks; jumping to a Task lands on its first page, even if that page is masked.

## State, stale and reopening

### Part state plus applicability

| Answered? | Applicability | Completion counts it? | Export |
|---|---|---|---|
| no | any | no | `result: null`, `applicability` |
| yes, not stale | active | yes | result, `stale: false` |
| yes, not stale | inactive | no | result, `stale: true` (it became stale on the transition) |
| yes, stale | active | no | result, `stale: true` |
| yes, stale | pending | no | result, `stale: true` |

The `stale` column is the one stored piece of this table. Applicability is derived. A part commit
clears `stale`; a reset/clear of the response clears it; an active → inactive transition sets it
when an answer exists.

### Write-time algorithm

Every part commit, reset and re-import that can change an answer or the part set runs, inside the
existing transaction:

1. Read the Task's parts and current responses; evaluate applicability **before**.
2. Apply the write (event, part state, reset, import change).
3. Evaluate applicability **after**.
4. For each part with an answer that moved active → inactive, set `stale = 1`.
5. For each required part that moved inactive → active while the Task is complete, reopen the Task
   and record the reason.
6. Recompute automatic completion over active required parts (not stale).
7. Keep an explicit completion unless step 5 reopened it.

The write is atomic: a failed validation writes nothing, marks nothing stale and does not reopen.

### Reopening and the completion control

- The explicit completion control still completes with pending active required parts after a
  confirmation that names the count.
- The control's count excludes inactive parts and includes stale parts.
- A reopened Task reads as incomplete; the reactivated part's page says why it is answerable again.
  `task_state.completionSource = "reopened"` records the transition, and no `review_events` row is
  appended — the history of answers stays a history of answers.
- The List's completion transition follows the Task's state: a List that was complete becomes
  incomplete when a member Task reopens, and completes again when all members complete again.

## Protocol v3 in detail

The full grammar, limits and diagnostics freeze in the plan. The design-level points:

- `protocol: "listea.package/3"`.
- v3 is v2 plus `prompts[].when`, `kind: "media"` and `response: "freeform"` for that kind.
- Conditions are manifest-level declarations, one per Prompt, acyclic by earlier index.
- `when` has `prompt`, `field`, and exactly one of `equals`/`in`; values are strings within the
  existing limits.
- A condition's source field is validated through the source Viewer's capability; Core does not
  parse a Viewer body to learn its fields.
- A media body is `{"stem": "…"}`; the stem is required and bounded; the Prompt has at least one
  file and no options.
- v1/v2 compatibility is unchanged. The v3-only words are rejected under v2 with their own codes;
  the v0.8.4 reader rejects a v3 manifest as an unknown protocol.
- The fingerprint includes conditions, so a condition edit is a content change.

## Schema 34 in detail

```sql
ALTER TABLE task_parts ADD COLUMN condition TEXT;
ALTER TABLE task_parts ADD COLUMN partJudgment TEXT;
ALTER TABLE task_part_state ADD COLUMN stale INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 0;
```

Semantics:

- `condition` is the canonical condition JSON, null for an unconditional part. It is read fail-closed:
  an unreadable condition makes the part unavailable, not active.
- `partJudgment` is a template reference, null for inheritance. It is read fail-closed: an
  unresolvable reference makes the part unavailable.
- `stale` is written only by the applicability-aware write path. It is false for a part with no
  answer.
- The migration is additive and does not rewrite old rows. A schema-33 Task has no conditions and
  no judgments, so its behavior, page list and v6 export are unchanged.
- Dissolution records gain the condition and judgment (and stale) in a versioned part record; an old
  record reads as unconditional, inheriting and fresh.

## Import and reconciliation

- The ImportPlan carries each part's condition and resolved judgment.
- A Package with conditions recommends **one Task per Section**; the import dialog says why. The
  prompt composition can still be chosen, but a conditioned part whose source lands in another Task
  imports unavailable with `condition-source-outside-task` and the report names it. Re-importing
  with a closed composition repairs the availability.
- A condition source removed by a new revision detaches; the dependent parts become unavailable
  (`condition-source-detached`) and block if required. The producer is expected to update or remove
  the dependent Prompt at the same time; the app does not guess an unconditional meaning.
- A condition or judgment change is part of the content fingerprint. The existing keep/reset/drop
  policy applies; after the run, applicability is recomputed and stale/reopen follow.
- The add-on is untouched by conditions and judgments.

It must include at least:

1. One Package whose Task contains a yes/no gate and a **PDF review** part, imported with an add-on.
2. The same shape with an **image/video review** part.
3. The same shape with a **vocabulary** part.
4. The same shape with a **dating/profile** part.
5. At least one **non-yes/no** task beyond the questionnaire: a media part, a folder-of-images part,
   or a multi-field form.
6. One Task mixing several of the above, imported with an add-on, with the add-on page after the
   parts.
7. All browse and review flows show the correct **Task** count and page count: Todo preview, List
   item preview, Quick Review, List Review, catalog, import report and queue menu/jump.
8. Changing the gate from yes to no masks the conditional pages with an explanation card, keeps the
   page count, keeps the Task count, and does not lose the answers.
9. Changing the gate back reactivates the parts, marks their answers stale, reopens a completed Task,

10. Export v7 carries each part's condition, applicability, stale flag and judgment; a v0.8.4 Task
    still exports v6 and a single-part media Task still exports v5.
11. A one-Task-per-question composition that splits a condition is reported and fails closed.
12. Schema 33 → 34 migrates an existing database with no change to old answers, pages or v6 bytes.

## Deviations and decisions taken during implementation

Recorded here because each one narrows the design or the plan; the topic docs linked from the row
carry the behaviour in full.

1. **The media-part judgment names the payload Viewer, not the asset Viewer.** `builtin.media.part@1`
   declares `builtin.viewer.media` — the freeform media Prompt — rather than
   `builtin.media.viewer`, the file viewer. Naming the file viewer drew the Done and then refused
   the commit, because the page and the answer were checked against two different definitions. The
   behavior is in [Templates → Part judgments](../templates.md#part-judgments-and-the-mixed-container).
2. **Applicability is re-derived on every read, write, import and export, never read from a stored
   answer.** A source's answer can move without any write to the dependent part, and a stored
   applicability would be a second fact to keep in sync. The evaluator is shared by the page list,
   completion and the export, which is what keeps them from disagreeing.
3. **A committed part's Done advances past that part's remaining Asset pages.** The assets were
   judged under the part, and walking each one after Done would ask the person to re-read pages the
   part already covers. The advance lands on the next part's first page.
4. **Stale reconfirmation under a non-revisable judgment is unit-tested, not replayed on the
   phone.** The walk used a revisable single-choice judgment; the non-revisable reconfirmation rule
   (a second answer stays refused, the one reconfirmation the branch change requires is allowed) is
   pinned in `PartRevisabilityTest`.
5. **A reopen is marked, not logged.** It writes `task_state.completionSource = 'reopened'` and
   appends no `review_events` row. Any later submission writes its own event; the reopen itself was
   not the person's answer.
6. **Recovery restores completion, and only completion.** `writeRecoveredState` copies
   `isCompleted` verbatim from the dissolved record; it does not replay `review_events` or keep
   `completionSource`, and it does not recompute completion against the new composition. This is the
   documented meaning of the keep-state option, not a compute path; the walk's step 8 is the
   evidence (`isCompleted = 0`, `completionSource = ''`, zero events).
7. **A refresh keeps the source name, grouping and composition; only a fresh Create Tasks offers
   the composition choice.** A second Todo over the same folder is allowed. This is the existing
   refresh rule, and it is why a condition source moved by a refresh is diagnosed as unavailable
   rather than silently repaired.
8. **A Package Todo's Tasks do not appear as List rows until they are added to a List.** They are
   catalog Tasks; the acceptance step 7 counted them in the Todo preview, Quick Review and the
   queue, and a List shows them only when that List holds them.
9. **The Todo Task preview draws the raw payload, not the stored choice.** The Gate therefore read
   as unanswered in the Todo preview while Quick Review drew the recovered **Yes**; the preview is a
   read of the content, not of the answer sheet. Recorded as a known presentation difference, not a
   data difference — the answer is in the database and in the export.
10. **The v0.8.4 fixtures keep their old versions.** The dating List is a single structured part and
    exports v5; the section-demo List holds several parts and exports v6. The first walk expected
    v6 for the dating file; the single-part rule, not the build, was the answer, and both fixtures
    correctly stayed unchanged.
11. **A v7 body has no availability key.** An unavailable target — a conditioned part whose source
    the Package no longer contains — exports as `applicability: "inactive"` with its `when` naming a
    source that is not in the body. A receiver that needs to tell "not asked" from "cannot be
    asked" has only the diagnostic history on the device; the wire says what was asked.
12. **`ExportPartView.unreadable` selects the version and never appears in the body.** A stored
    condition or judgment this build cannot decode forces v7 so the round is not silently demoted
    to a version that has no spelling for a branch — but v7 has none either. The undecoded object
    is written as `null`, and applicability is evaluated from the parts' readable conditions, so
    an unreadable condition exports `applicability: "active"`, indistinguishable on the wire from
    an unconditional part. The phone still refuses the page through the availability diagnostic
    (`condition-invalid`/`unsupported-judgment`), which has no wire key. Pinned by `ExportV7Test`
    `an unreadable condition exports null when and still claims v7`.
13. **The recovered Task showed eleven pages, not twelve.** The fresh Todo's add-on was left at
    *None*, so the add-on page was absent; the recovered answers and the part pages were otherwise
    intact. An add-on is a Todo choice, not a Task-level decision the kept history restores.

## What does not change

- The Add-on: Task-level, optional, after the parts, never conditional, never part of completion.
- Media/PDF viewing, marks, pair state, asset actions, rotation, and the page-arrow semantics
  (`<< >>` Task-level, `<>` Asset-level).
- The queue model: a queue holds Tasks; a Task's pages are internal.
- List membership, source import, file/folder identity, resume, Quick Review/List Review queues.
- Templates and Viewer contracts, except the new media Viewer, the media-part judgment, the mixed
  container and the part judgment column.
- Export history: old documents remain readable; v1/v2 Packages remain readable.
- Schema 33 data: no response, event, Asset or composition is reinterpreted.

## Open items

- **Cross-Task conditions.** A condition whose source is in another Task is still out of scope.
- **Compound conditions.** One source field and `equals`/`in`; no AND/OR over several sources.
  Chains compose; a boolean expression does not.
- **Conditions on media/freeform answers.** Freeform has no named scalar; a media part cannot be a
  source. It can be a target.
- **Optional sources.** A condition source must be required.
- **A per-part template picker.** v0.8.5 resolves built-ins; a person cannot yet override one part's
  judgment in the dialog. The column and export are ready for it.
- **Dynamic queue insertion.** Reopening a Task affects the next queue, not an open round.
- **Conditional add-ons**, conditional Sections, and custom compositions that compute closure.
- **Editing conditions on the device.** The Package remains the setter's declaration.
- **A `.listea` archive**, webhook/n8n grading, and a general expression language.
